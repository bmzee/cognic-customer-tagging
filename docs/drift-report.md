# Tag drift report (synthetic example)

Generated by `tools/drift.py` from the prevalence the builder records with each run. Drift is attribute-blind: it shows that a tag's prevalence moved, not why, and it is never a fairness finding. A flag is a reason for the taxonomy owner to look (see the `tag-review` skill).

Example: the 300 synthetic customers in `data/`, built into a store at the end of March 2026 and then followed month by month to September (one batch of the month's transactions and one month-end tick each), so the store recorded seven runs. Regenerate with `python3 tools/drift.py demo --out docs/drift-report.md`.

The synthetic data is not a steady population: its history starts in October 2025, and its labelled scenarios are built around the end of September 2026. Tags that need a long history appear only from mid-2026, and patterns built for September look different earlier. The flags below come from how the data was made, not from customers changing; that is exactly the kind of cause the report cannot tell apart from a real change, which is why a flag asks a person to look.

Method: the baseline is the mean of the first 3 recorded runs after each taxonomy change, then frozen. WARN at a 25% relative change of at least 1 point; ALERT at 50% and at least 2 points; both need a two-proportion z-test p-value below 0.01. A tag falling to zero when at least 5 customers were expected to hold it, or the population changing by 20% or more, is an ALERT. The spread of tags per customer is compared with the population stability index (WARN 0.10, ALERT 0.25), shown beside the value chance alone would exceed one time in a hundred.

## Limitations

- Runs of one store describe the same customers, so they are not independent samples; the z-test treats them as if they were and is a screening aid, not a proof.
- A change can come from the customers (a season, a new product, a closed branch), from the data feed (a missing file, a new category code) or from the rules. The report cannot tell which.
- Small stores move a lot by chance; a tag held by few customers rarely reaches significance.
- A rebuilt store keeps only its latest run: keep earlier stores to see a trend.
